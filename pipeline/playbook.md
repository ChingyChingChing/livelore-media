# Live Lore Shorts playbook

How to make a Short that people watch to the end. The daily task reads this with strategy.md before writing each script.
strategy.md = what OUR data says (wins when they disagree). playbook.md = what research on short-form video says.
The weekly task refreshes this file. Last updated: 2026-11-08 (first version, research only, no channel data yet).

## What YouTube rewards (how distribution works)
- Each Short is first shown to a small test audience; if they watch and don't swipe, it is pushed wider. "The audience is the algorithm." (YouTube Shorts lead Todd Sherman, Creator Insider 2023, via OutlierKit; vidIQ 2026)
- Key metric: "viewed vs swiped away" in YouTube Studio (Analytics > Content > Shorts). A high swipe rate = weak first seconds or wrong audience. (vidIQ 2026, YouTube Help)
- Only "engaged views" (watching past the first seconds) count toward YPP; every start and loop counts as a plain view. (vidIQ 2026, YouTube blog)
- Since 1 Oct 2026 YouTube further prioritises original content and cuts reach of re-uploads. Our original graphics + own script are an advantage; keep it that way. (YouTube via OutlierKit)
- Posting time, hashtags and thumbnails matter little in the Shorts feed. (Sherman via OutlierKit; vidIQ)
- Staying in one niche helps the system find the right viewers. (vidIQ 2026)

## Hook (0-3 s)
- Most drop-off happens in the first 3 seconds (one guide: 50-60% of all drop-offs). Target: 70%+ still watching after 3 s. (OpusClip; vidIQ)
- Open on the payoff or the strangest fact, not on context. No intro, no channel name, no "today we...".
- First frame must already show motion and big on-screen text matching the first spoken line.
- Patterns to rotate and test (log which one each Short used): surprising number ("96 on Metacritic... and it still lost"), contradiction ("A shop that begs you not to buy"), direct question ("Would you pay $7 for this?"), "nobody is talking about..." (Asia angle).

## Holding attention
- New visual every 2-4 s (we do 2-3 s: keep). (OpusClip)
- Burned-in captions (linked to 15-25% higher retention in one guide; we already do this). (OpusClip)
- Plant a reason to stay early: a tease of the twist/number that is paid off later ("...and the twist is at the end" or an open question).
- Cut dead air, slow intros and delayed payoffs. Trim the first-draft script by ~30%. (vidIQ; OpusClip)
- Watch for a retention cliff (a 30-40% drop at one moment) in Studio: that sentence or scene is the problem. (OpusClip)

## Length
- No ideal length per YouTube: "to what length do you need to tell your story?" (Sherman)
- Guides suggest 15-30 s keeps the highest retention and that drop-off rises past ~45 s unless the content is strong; one large study found 13 s and 60 s Shorts did best. (OpusClip; vidIQ citing Nate Black) Evidence is mixed and mostly unverified.
- Our Shorts run ~50 s. TEST: alternate ~35-40 s and ~50 s versions and let the weekly analytics decide (compare retention %, not raw views).

## Ending and loop
- End fast after the payoff; the last line can flow back into the first (loops count as views and signal rewatch value).
- Keep one question CTA ("Remake or original?") to invite comments; keep "Full breakdown in Sunday's recap".
- Set each Short's related video to the latest long recap once those exist. (vidIQ)
