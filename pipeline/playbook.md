# Live Lore Shorts playbook

How to make a Short that people watch to the end. The daily task reads this with strategy.md before writing each script.
strategy.md = what OUR data says (wins when they disagree). playbook.md = what research on short-form video says.
The weekly task refreshes this file. Last updated: 2026-11-23 (research only; still no per-video channel data).

## What YouTube rewards (how distribution works)
- Each Short is first shown to a small test audience; if they watch and don't swipe, it is pushed wider. "The audience is the algorithm." (YouTube Shorts lead Todd Sherman, Creator Insider 2023, via OutlierKit; vidIQ 2026)
- Key metric: "viewed vs swiped away" in YouTube Studio (Analytics > Content > Shorts). A high swipe rate = weak first seconds or wrong audience. (vidIQ 2026, YouTube Help)
- Only "engaged views" (watching past the first seconds) count toward YPP; every start and loop counts as a plain view. (vidIQ 2026, YouTube blog)
- Other 2026 changes that touched recommendations were few: teen recommendations (Jan 14), push notifications to inactive subscribers (Apr 21), and the Oct 1 Shorts originality update. A Jul 3 Creator Insider item changed how two viewer controls are counted, with YouTube saying recommendations did not change. (AIR Media-Tech month-by-month tracker, vendor summary of YouTube posts)
- Since Oct 2026 YouTube cuts Shorts reach for channels that re-upload others' clips with minimal edits or just a voiceover, and says it wants to help original creators grow. Confirmed by YouTube statements in several outlets. Our original graphics + own script are an advantage; never build a Short around someone else's footage. (Android Headlines, Oct 2026; MediaPost; PPC Land)
- Freshness: analysts report Shorts older than ~30 days now get fewer views and new uploads are favoured. YouTube has NOT confirmed this. If true it suits a daily news channel: post fresh stories, don't count on old Shorts picking up later. (Search Engine Journal; Tubefilter) UNVERIFIED.
- Posting time, hashtags and thumbnails matter little in the Shorts feed. (Sherman via OutlierKit; vidIQ)
- Staying in one niche helps the system find the right viewers. (vidIQ 2026)
- Metricool's 2026 study (799,718 videos, 71,177 accounts): 61% of measured views now come from the Shorts feed, and channels posting ~5 Shorts/month got 46% more views than the study average. CORRECTION (2026-11-23): the "-37% average view duration" figure is for long-form; for Shorts, coverage says average watch time per view fell to about 16 s (from ~48 s in 2025), a drop of 63–67% (the two summaries disagree). Large sample, but correlation, not proof. (Metricool YouTube Trends 2026; Metricool press release; Tubefilter, Aug 2026; Journalism UK)
- Takeaway for us: if the typical Short view lasts ~16 s, our 50–55 s Shorts need a reason to stay at ~10–15 s (a mid-point twist or open question), not just a strong first 3 s. Inference, not tested.

## Hook (0-3 s)
- Most drop-off happens in the first 3 seconds (one guide: 50-60% of all drop-offs). Target: 70%+ still watching after 3 s. (OpusClip; vidIQ)
- Open on the payoff or the strangest fact, not on context. No intro, no channel name, no "today we...".
- First frame must already show motion and big on-screen text matching the first spoken line.
- Make the hook work on mute: the first on-screen text should carry the hook alone. One creator says 40% of their weak videos had hooks that failed with sound off (anecdotal, UNVERIFIED), but many viewers scroll muted, so it costs nothing. (dev.to hook-engineering write-up; vidIQ hooks guide)
- "Curiosity gap" (hold back the key detail) and "counter-claim" ("everyone thinks X; actually Y") are the hook types vendor guides rate most reliable. That fits our contradiction and surprising_number hooks. Their retention numbers (e.g. "68% 3-second hold") have no published method: UNVERIFIED. (vidIQ "Viral hooks" 2026; OpusClip hook formulas; Blitzcut)
- Patterns to rotate and test (log which one each Short used): surprising number ("96 on Metacritic... and it still lost"), contradiction ("A shop that begs you not to buy"), direct question ("Would you pay $7 for this?"), "nobody is talking about..." (Asia angle).

## Holding attention
- New visual every 2-4 s (we do 2-3 s: keep). (OpusClip)
- Burned-in captions (linked to 15-25% higher retention in one guide; we already do this). (OpusClip)
- Plant a reason to stay early: a tease of the twist/number that is paid off later ("...and the twist is at the end" or an open question).
- Cut dead air, slow intros and delayed payoffs. Trim the first-draft script by ~30%. (vidIQ; OpusClip)
- Watch for a retention cliff (a 30-40% drop at one moment) in Studio: that sentence or scene is the problem. (OpusClip)
- Judge each Short against OUR channel's own baseline ("compared to your typical performance" in Studio), not against outside benchmarks: published swipe/stayed-to-watch "healthy" numbers (e.g. 10–30% swipe-away, 80% hold at 3 s) are vendor rules of thumb and conflict with each other. UNVERIFIED. (Humbleandbrag; Bytecap; 1of10)
- "Stayed to watch" measures the choice to keep watching at the start, not completion; read it together with avg view duration. (ContentStudio, citing YouTube docs)

## Length
- No ideal length per YouTube: "to what length do you need to tell your story?" (Sherman)
- Guides suggest 15-30 s keeps the highest retention and that drop-off rises past ~45 s unless the content is strong; one large study found 13 s and 60 s Shorts did best. (OpusClip; vidIQ citing Nate Black) Evidence is mixed and mostly unverified.
- Adobe Express survey of 507 creators (1k+ subs): 46% think under 30 s is best for views/shares; for subscriber growth, under-30 s and 30–60 s tied at 34%. This is opinion, not measured retention. (Adobe Express, 2026)
- One 2026 guide suggests testing ~15–35 s first, then letting your own retention data pick a length (vidIQ "get more views" 2026; vendor, unverified). Our test (35–40 s vs ~50 s) is a fair version of that.
- Our Shorts 1–16 ran 41–55 s (15 of 16 at 45–55 s). TEST (strategy.md, from Short 17): odd Shorts ~35–40 s, even ~48–52 s; weekly analytics decides by retention %, not raw views.

## Ending and loop
- End fast after the payoff; the last line can flow back into the first (loops count as views and signal rewatch value).
- Loop rate, swipe-away rate and shares are named by vendor guides as top Shorts signals; YouTube has not published a ranking of signals, so treat this as opinion. (Fliki; dev.to; YouTube has "never published a detailed breakdown")
- Keep one question CTA ("Remake or original?") to invite comments; keep "Full breakdown in Sunday's recap".
- Set each Short's related video to the latest long recap once those exist. (vidIQ)

## Length limit and YPP routes (checked 2026-11-09)
- Shorts can be up to 3 minutes since Oct 2024; no official ideal length, retention matters more than runtime. Vendor blogs say ~20-45 s is a sweet spot; none is from YouTube, so treat as weak/unverified. (Toptal, miraflow, gofaceless via search; Search Engine Journal)
- Shorts YPP route is 1,000 subscribers + 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days; long-form route is 1,000 subs + 4,000 (full-tier 8,000 in our goal) watch hours. Shorts-feed watch time does NOT count toward the long-form hours, so the hours route needs long videos (e.g. the weekly recap). One guide says an Aug 2026 YPP update sets 1 Feb 2027 for a change to Shorts revenue-sharing eligibility at 10M views/90 days; single vendor source (Fliki), UNVERIFIED, do not act on it. One unconfirmed report (Notebookcheck) of a stricter 2027 bar: UNVERIFIED.
- Since 31 Mar 2025 a Shorts view counts on play/replay with no minimum; "engaged views" is the metric tied to YPP. (SEJ, TechCrunch)
- Open loops / escalating structure are said to lift mid-video retention (aibrify, vendor blog; weak). A 20 s Short at 85% retention beats a 60 s at 50%: judge by retention %.
